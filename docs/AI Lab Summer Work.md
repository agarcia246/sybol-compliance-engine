**The Problem**

Media authenticity is increasingly difficult to verify. Deepfakes and AI-generated content are getting harder to distinguish from real images and video, and right now there's no standardized, machine-readable way to prove where a piece of media came from or whether it was generated by an AI.

This creates real problems — for platforms that need to moderate content, for regulators trying to enforce EU law around AI-generated media, and for anyone who needs to trust what they're seeing. The legal landscape is also moving fast: the EU AI Act, GDPR, and the Digital Product Passport framework all have something to say about this, but nobody has connected them yet into a working technical system.

The gap we're filling: there's no tool today that takes a piece of media, evaluates its authenticity against EU regulation, and issues a cryptographically verifiable, machine-readable proof of that evaluation. That's what we're building.

**What We're Building**

A Compliance AI Engine with three interconnected parts:

| Part 1 — Media Authenticity Scoring |
| :---- |

The engine takes an image as input and runs it through four independent signal extractors:

* m — Metadata integrity: checks for missing EXIF fields, editing software tags (Photoshop, GIMP, Stable Diffusion), and timestamp anomalies

* a — Generative artifact detection: uses a CNN-based deepfake detector, FFT frequency analysis, and noise residual analysis to catch AI-generated patterns

* v — Visual consistency: checks lighting uniformity, shadow direction, and edge blending artifacts via OpenCV — facial landmarks are explicitly excluded for GDPR compliance

* p — Provenance: compares a perceptual hash of the image against a known authentic dataset — no third-party reverse lookup to avoid GDPR joint controllership issues

These four signals are assembled into a feature vector X \= \[m, a, v, p\] and fed into a weighted scoring function, calibrated with Platt scaling. The output is a float between 0.0 and 1.0:

| Score range | Compliance status | What it means |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **0.0 – 0.3** | Non-compliant | Almost certainly AI-generated or deepfake — fails EU AI Act Art. 50(2) disclosure requirements |
| **0.3 – 0.7** | Review | Partially authentic or edited — human-in-the-loop required, no automated decision (GDPR Art. 22\) |
| **0.7 – 1.0** | Compliant | Authentic media — passes all signal checks and provenance verification |

| Part 2 — RAG Compliance Engine |
| :---- |

Behind every score is a RAG pipeline that ingests EU regulation documents and retrieves the specific rules and article citations that justify the result. This means every output is explainable and legally grounded — not just a number.

The pipeline ingests five regulation sources:

* EU AI Act — obligations for providers of AI systems, transparency requirements for synthetic content (Art. 50), prohibited practices around biometrics (Art. 5\)

* GDPR — data minimisation, DPIA obligations, rights of data subjects in automated decision-making (Art. 22), biometric data classification (Art. 4(14))

* ESPR / Digital Product Passport (EU 2024/1781) — framework for attaching machine-readable identity and compliance data to a product

* LOPDGDD — Spanish implementation of GDPR with stricter biometric processing requirements and AEPD enforcement posture

* Ley 13/2022 — Spanish audiovisual law covering AI-generated content disclosure obligations for platforms

The RAG engine chunks documents at article level, embeds them locally with sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (no external API calls — fully GDPR-safe), stores them in Qdrant, and synthesizes results with Mistral Large. The output is a structured regulationRefs array — regulation name, article number, and source URL — that feeds directly into the Verifiable Credential.

| Part 3 — Verifiable Credential Issuance |
| :---- |

The score and all supporting data get encoded into a W3C VC Data Model 2.0 Verifiable Credential — a cryptographically signed, tamper-proof document that acts as a Digital Product Passport for the media asset. It's issued via Sybol's digital identity infrastructure (DID \+ svault cryptographic layer) and includes:

* The authenticity score and per-signal breakdown \[m, a, v, p\]

* Compliance status and the regulation articles that justify it

* A SHA-256 hash of the original media file for traceability

* A link to the Qdrant audit trail record

* Model version and analysis timestamp for reproducibility

* A StatusList2021 revocation entry so erroneous credentials can be retracted

The credential is machine-readable, verifiable by any W3C-compliant system, and doesn't require raw image data to be stored — only the hash and feature signals, in line with GDPR data minimisation principles.

**Why AI makes sense here**

* Pattern detection at scale — generative artifacts and subtle manipulations are sub-perceptual. A CNN-based detector catches frequency domain signals that no rule-based system can reliably identify.

* Dynamic regulatory knowledge — the RAG approach means the system stays current with regulation without retraining. When the AI Act implementing acts update, we re-ingest the PDFs.

* Explainability by design — the per-signal breakdown and regulation citations make every output auditable, which directly satisfies GDPR Art. 22 transparency requirements and the AI Act's limited-risk provider obligations.

* Verifiable, not just readable — encoding the result as a W3C VC makes it machine-verifiable by any compliant system, not just a PDF report someone has to manually check.
